Hair fall in women: common causes and what helps

Thinning in women usually has a cause that can be found. Here are the most common ones and how they are treated.

Hair loss in women rarely looks like male baldness. It is more often a widening parting, a thinner ponytail or more hair in the brush than usual. The good news is that a cause can usually be found, and many of them are treatable.

Common causes

  • After pregnancy: hormone changes after delivery push many hairs into the shedding phase at once. It usually settles within a year.
  • Low iron or vitamin levels: low ferritin, vitamin D or B12 are frequent findings in women with diffuse thinning.
  • Thyroid problems: both an underactive and an overactive thyroid can cause hair fall.
  • Hormonal conditions such as PCOS: these can cause thinning on top together with other symptoms.
  • Female pattern hair loss: a gradual, inherited thinning over the crown and parting.
  • Stress, illness or crash dieting: a shock to the body can trigger heavy shedding two to three months later.

How it is diagnosed

A good consultation starts with your history, then a close look at the scalp with a trichoscope. Blood tests are suggested when the pattern or your symptoms point to an internal cause.

What helps

Treating the cause comes first, such as correcting iron levels or managing thyroid function. On top of that, topical or oral medicines, PRP and GFC can strengthen weak follicles and reduce shedding. A transplant is an option for stable, localised thinning, usually without shaving.

If you have noticed more hair fall than usual for over three months, it is worth booking a consultation rather than waiting.
